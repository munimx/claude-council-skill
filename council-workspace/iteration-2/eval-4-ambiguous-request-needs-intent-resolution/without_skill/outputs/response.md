I can't give you a verdict yet. Your message doesn't say what either approach is, and a council has to know what it's voting on. Any answer I gave now would be a guess.

**What I need (a few lines on each is enough):**

1. **What you have now:** what it is, and what's actually wrong with it. A specific pain like "deploys take 40 minutes" or "this breaks every release" counts more than a general feeling that it could be better.
2. **The new approach:** what it is, and what specifically it would fix.
3. **Why this is coming up now:** did something break, did someone propose it, or did you come across a new tool?
4. **What switching costs:** time, migration work, retraining people, and the risk of a half-finished switch.
5. **How reversible it is:** can you try it on one part first, or is it all or nothing?
6. **Evidence:** have you tested the new approach against your real workload, or only read about it?
7. **Which way you're leaning, and why.** This lets me check your reasoning instead of just agreeing with you.

**Until then, the default rule is:** keep what you have unless the new approach fixes a real, specific problem that the current one can't fix with a smaller change, and the gain is clearly bigger than the switching cost.

Three things usually decide it:

- **Pain you can name:** if you can't say what's wrong with the current approach in one sentence, keep it.
- **A cheap trial:** if you can try the new approach on one slice with an easy way back, do that before committing.
- **Deciding on novelty:** "it's newer or cleaner" is the most common reason teams switch, and the one they most often regret.
